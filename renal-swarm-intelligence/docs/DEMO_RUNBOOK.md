# Renal Swarm Intelligence demo runbook

## Audience promise

In fifteen minutes, show a governed enterprise intelligence and business outcome harness—not another alert dashboard or generic AI copilot.

## Before presenting

- Use only the included synthetic Riverbend patient and facility records.
- Say that labeled CMS, CDC, USRDS and eCQI sources and public benchmark values are real.
- Say that live EMR writes and CMS/EQRS/NHSN transmission are disabled.
- Open Swarm Control once so the D1 runtime can materialize the synthetic replay if the ledger is empty.
- Treat every highlighted card, metric, event row, topology object, work item, facility station, CMS gap and assurance trace as a drill-down. The shared inspector exposes Overview, Evidence and Activity, then continues into the accountable cockpit.

## Fifteen-minute path

1. **Platform Admin · 150 sec.** Save the tenant boundary, open Kafka setup and explain why the organization-owned bridge sits beside its brokers. Save only a binding reference, run the labeled contract test, edit one agent or policy threshold, run all five release suites, then activate. Point to draft → validated → active, the rollback target and **Code redeploy: Not required**. Do not describe a reference-host contract check as live broker verification.
2. **Agent Operations · 75 sec.** Inspect the edited active manifest, its typed triggers, allowed proposals, eval gate, kill switch and dedicated message fabric. Pause an agent in a later draft to show that configuration is explicit and reversible.
3. **Swarm Control · 120 sec.** Reset, step and play the persisted event replay. Watch D1 evidence, cell runs, swarm insights, explicit conflicts and ranked NBAs appear. Switch DVP/ROD/FA scope, inspect a manifest, move the threshold and point out that the server replays persisted events in an isolated policy sandbox.
4. **Outcome Command · 75 sec.** A synthetic discharge, two exact assessment answers and facility capacity become one continuity episode. Open the work-item inspector to show the closed-loop rail and exact activity. Use **Request coordinator review** to retain a durable human handoff. Approve the Class B NBA: a command enters the durable Kafka outbox. Click again to simulate a downstream acknowledgement; the episode resolves and a measure result is calculated.
5. **Assessment Intelligence · 60 sec.** Show the exact D1 answer, source event, valid/recorded time and hash. Record a human evidence review. Explain that embedded instructions remain untrusted content and cannot become commands.
6. **Shared Intelligence · 75 sec.** Switch enterprise, patient, regulatory and swarm projections. Inspect event/source nodes and typed edges, then attach a versioned, scope-authorized institutional note to a node.
7. **Facility Operations · 45 sec.** Run the server-side safe scenario. It resolves persisted chair/machine, staff headroom and cited afternoon-preference evidence, but changes no schedule or order.
8. **CMS Operations · 75 sec.** Show real source links, synthetic separation and exact CMS Provider Data benchmark. Generate a content-addressed D1 dry-run package. It stops at dual approval; do not call it a live submission.
9. **AI Assurance · 75 sec.** Execute prompt injection, cross-patient contamination, duplicate delivery, stale-source and unsafe-action red tests. Show the returned control assertions and evidence hash, then inspect actual runtime traces, drift snapshots, cost and outbox state.
10. **Executive Outcomes · 45 sec.** Show the four-outcome portfolio, hierarchy scorecard and signal-to-verified-outcome value flow.
11. **Configuration Studio · 45 sec.** Show organization hierarchy, role rights, eleven renal domain packs, adapter/Kafka contracts, 12 active reference cells, policies, sources, thresholds and measure packs as independently versioned configuration.

## Close

> Renal Swarm Intelligence turns the renal-care enterprise into a governed, event-native intelligence system—from patient voice and facility operations to regulatory readiness and verified business outcomes.

## Precise answers

**Autonomous clinical AI?** No. It is bounded decision support and coordination. Clinical/high-risk actions require authorized humans and source-system controls.

**Real patient data?** No. Patient, treatment, staff and facility operations are synthetic. Labeled federal authority data are real public sources.

**Live CMS submission?** The foundation prepares, validates, signs and audits a package. Transmission stays disabled until credentials, connector certification and dual approval exist.

**Why Kafka?** It preserves the existing event-first architecture, enables replay/correction and avoids a point-to-point AI integration mesh.

**Why a hypergraph?** A first-class relation can carry patient scope, time, source, purpose, policy and confidence across evidence, proposals, actions and outcomes.

**Why 2039-ready?** Schema versions, bitemporal state, effective-date snapshots, independent measure packs, compatibility gates, replay fixtures and retained rollback targets.

**Does “no redeploy” mean no governance?** No. Code stays unchanged, but the content-addressed configuration release still needs schema, green, red, integration and promotion evidence plus role-specific approval. A production environment also requires live bridge verification.
